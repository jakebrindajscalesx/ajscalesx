# OFM Mentorship — Business Plan
**Product:** "Zero to Agency" — an OFM (OnlyFans Management) business course
**Model:** Low-ticket digital info-product, sold via Instagram organic content → Skool checkout
**Price:** $97 one-time (anchor at $197, "founding cohort" discount to $97)
**Face of the brand:** Your mate (the operator) — he's the proof, you run the business/marketing/ops engine

---

## 1. What business are you actually in?

Be precise about this because it changes what you can legally advertise and where:

- You are **not** selling adult content and you are **not** an OF creator.
- You are selling **education/consulting on running a social-media-management agency** — specifically, one vertical (subscription-platform creator management: chatting, growth, content scheduling, monetization strategy).
- That's the same category as "how to start a marketing agency," "how to become a VA," "how to run a Twitch/YouTube management agency." It's a legitimate, teachable service business. Treat it with the same seriousness as any other agency-training product — because the compliance risk isn't "is OFM legal" (managing creators' business operations is legal), it's **platform ToS + payment processor ToS + honest marketing**, which is where most course-sellers in this niche get shut down.

## 2. Two revenue streams (don't confuse them)

| Stream | What it is | Who runs it |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The agency** | Your mate (+ team) manages OF creators for a % of their earnings (typically 20-50% rev share) | Your mate, ongoing |
| **B. The course** | Teaching other people how to build stream A themselves | You (business/marketing side) + your mate (credibility/content/curriculum) |

Keep these as **separate offers with separate pricing**, even if cross-promoted. Stream A is a service business with client contracts; Stream B is a productized info-product. Don't blend the books — you'll want stream A's revenue as your "proof" (screenshots, case studies) but the course itself is sold and delivered independently of any specific agency client relationship.

## 3. Roles

- **Your mate:** subject-matter expert, on-camera talent, curriculum content (what actually works day to day), answers student questions / does the occasional live Q&A, lends credibility ("I run X accounts, made $Y last quarter").
- **You:** business structure, course build, sales page, Skool setup, Instagram content system, ad/organic growth, customer support, refunds, legal paperwork, analytics.
- **Split:** decide this explicitly before launch — e.g., 50/50 on course profit, or your mate gets a flat fee/percentage while you own the "business" (Skool, page, brand) since you're building and running it. Put it in writing (see legal-compliance-checklist.md) even though you're mates — this is exactly the kind of thing that ends friendships if it's a handshake deal and the course actually sells.

## 4. Positioning & niche-down

"How to get into OFM" is too broad and sounds like a generic reseller course. Niche the promise down to something concrete and specific to what your mate actually does well, e.g.:
- "How I manage 4 OF accounts to $30k/mo combined without touching content creation" (chatting/growth angle)
- "The agency model: sign creators, hire chatters, keep 30%, scale to 10 accounts"
- "From $0 to your first signed creator in 30 days"

Pick ONE specific angle for the v1 course. You can expand later. Vague "learn OFM" courses are what saturate this niche and get ignored — a specific, credible, numbers-backed promise from a real operator is what sells.

## 6. Pricing rationale

- $97 one-time is deliberately low-ticket: low barrier to buy off a cold Instagram audience, high volume, minimal after-sale support burden (no coaching calls required at this price).
- Anchor against a $197 "regular price" so the $97 reads as a launch/founding-member deal — creates urgency without fake scarcity (only do this once; don't run a fake "ending tonight" countdown forever, that erodes trust and can violate advertising standards).
- Refund policy: 7-day no-questions refund. At $97 this costs you very little and kills most complaint/chargeback risk before it starts (chargebacks are a real threat to your Stripe/payment processor standing — see compliance checklist).

## 9. What would actually kill this business (avoid these)

- **Fake income claims.** "I made $50k last month" needs to be true and ideally screenshot-backed. Exaggerated earnings claims are the #1 reason "guru" courses get called out publicly and the #1 legal exposure (consumer/advertising law in most countries treats this as misleading conduct regardless of niche).
- **Promising the course = guaranteed income.** Frame it as education/skills, not a guaranteed-results scheme.
- **Marketing that violates Instagram/Meta ad policy.** Don't advertise adult content, don't use explicit imagery, don't imply you're selling access to adult content — you're selling a business-skills course. Organic content should read like any other "start an agency" creator (think: marketing/business content, screenshots of dashboards/earnings, talking-head advice), not adult-adjacent content.
- **No contract with your mate.** Formalize the split before money moves.
- **Treating creators (in the actual agency, stream A) badly.** If the course teaches how to sign and manage creators, the curriculum should model fair, consensual, transparent contracts — that's both the right thing to do and what protects the business's reputation long-term (see legal-compliance-checklist.md, section on creator contracts).
