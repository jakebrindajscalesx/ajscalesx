*How to build and run an OnlyFans Management agency — from your first signed creator to a scaled team.*

## Before you start: what this course is and isn't

This course teaches the **business and marketing operations** of managing subscription-platform creators — finding clients, negotiating deals, growing accounts, running the "chatting" side, pricing, contracts, and scaling a team. It assumes you (the student) are not the creator — you're the agency/manager.

It does not teach content creation, does not involve explicit material, and every practice taught here assumes fully consenting adult creators, platform-compliant operations, and honest business dealings. If any part of what you're doing doesn't fit that description, stop and fix that first — everything downstream depends on it.

## Module 1 — Understanding the OFM Business Model

**Lesson 1.1 — What an OFM agency actually does**
An OFM (OnlyFans Management) agency provides services to creators in exchange for a percentage of their subscription/tip revenue (commonly 20-50%, scaling with the size of the deal and services provided). Core services typically split into three buckets:
1. **Growth/marketing** — driving new subscribers via TikTok/Instagram/Reddit/X promo, SEO on other platforms, cross-promotion deals with other creators.
2. **Chatting/monetization** — messaging subscribers, running PPV (pay-per-view) content drops, building rapport, upselling.
3. **Operations** — scheduling posts, organizing content libraries, tracking analytics, handling admin so the creator can focus on creating.

Most agencies specialize early (usually chatting + growth) and add ops later as they scale.

**Lesson 1.2 — Why creators pay for this**
Creators are often good at content but don't have time/skill for 12-hour chatting shifts, sales psychology, or growth marketing. A good agency should demonstrably increase a creator's net income even after the rev-share cut — that's the entire value proposition. If you can't show a path to doing that, you don't have an offer yet.

**Lesson 1.3 — The realistic economics**
- A single well-managed mid-tier account might do $5k-$20k/month gross.
- At a 30% agency cut, that's $1,500-$6,000/month from one account.
- Agencies scale by managing multiple accounts simultaneously, not by squeezing more out of one.
- Chatters (if you hire them) are typically paid a base + commission per shift, so your margin is gross agency cut minus chatter wages minus tools/software.

**Module 1 action item:** Write down, in one paragraph, exactly which service(s) you personally will offer starting out (most beginners start with chatting + basic growth, not full-service).

## Module 2 — Finding & Signing Your First Creator

**Lesson 2.1 — Where to find creators looking for management**
- Twitter/X: search hashtags creators use to advertise ("content creator," "seeking manager"), engage genuinely before pitching.
- Instagram/TikTok: creators who post teaser content often list a Linktree — check if they mention wanting help.
- Direct, respectful outreach: a short message explaining who you are, what you offer, and asking if they're open to a chat. No cold hard-selling.
- Warm network: your mate's existing relationships/referrals are the highest-trust source when starting out.

**Lesson 2.2 — The discovery call**
Ask the creator about their current numbers (subscribers, monthly revenue, time spent), what they're struggling with (usually: time, chatting fatigue, inconsistent growth), and what a "win" looks like for them in 90 days. Listen more than you pitch on this call.

**Lesson 2.3 — Structuring the offer**
Common structures:
- **Straight rev-share**: e.g., 20% for growth-only, 30-40% for growth + chatting, 50% for full-service including content planning.
- **Trial period**: offer a 30-day trial at a lower cut to prove value before locking in a longer agreement.
- Always put the agreed % and scope in writing before touching the account (see contract template, Module 5).

**Lesson 2.4 — Red flags to walk away from**
- Creator wants you to pretend to be them in ways that mislead subscribers about consent/identity in harmful ways.
- Any indication the creator isn't verified as an adult on the platform, or won't confirm identity/age verification is complete.
- Unwillingness to sign any written agreement at all — a sign of future payment disputes.

**Module 2 action item:** Draft your discovery-call question list and your rev-share offer structure before reaching out to anyone.

## Module 3 — Content Strategy & Growth Marketing

**Lesson 3.1 — The funnel**
Free platforms (TikTok, Instagram, X, Reddit where permitted) → link in bio/Linktree → OF profile → free or low-cost subscription → upsell via PPV/tips. Your job as manager is optimizing every step of that funnel, not just the last one.

**Lesson 3.2 — Content cadence**
Consistency beats intensity. A realistic starting cadence: 1 post/day on the main platform, teaser clips 3-4x/week on free social, engaging with comments/DMs daily. Build a simple content calendar (see `course-dashboard.html` for a template you can adapt).

**Lesson 3.3 — Cross-promotion**
Partnering with other creators (shoutouts, collab content) is one of the fastest, cheapest growth levers — often more effective than paid ads at this stage, and it's how most serious agencies grow their second and third accounts (existing creators refer creators they trust).

**Lesson 3.4 — Analytics you should actually track**
- New subscriber count (weekly)
- Churn/cancellation rate
- Average revenue per subscriber
- PPV open/purchase rate
- Free-platform follower growth vs. conversion rate to paid

If you're not tracking these weekly, you're guessing, not managing.

**Module 3 action item:** Build a one-page weekly tracking sheet with the 5 metrics above for your first account.

## Module 4 — Chatting & Monetization

**Lesson 4.1 — The role of the chatter**
Chatting is relationship-building + sales, done at scale, across shifts. The best chatters read tone, personalize instead of copy-pasting, and know when to introduce a PPV without feeling pushy.

**Lesson 4.2 — PPV strategy**
Pay-per-view drops work best when: they're teased in advance, priced consistently (subscribers learn to trust the pricing), and timed around subscriber activity patterns (check analytics for peak online times).

**Lesson 4.3 — Shift structure (once you scale to hiring chatters)**
- Define shift blocks (e.g., 4-hour shifts covering different time zones for 24hr coverage).
- Use a shared response/tone guide per creator so chatting feels consistent even across different chatters.
- Track each chatter's sales performance (revenue generated per shift) to know who to schedule more.

**Lesson 4.4 — Boundaries and consent**
The creator sets the boundaries for what chatters can/can't say or offer on their behalf — get this in writing, review it together, and never let a chatter improvise past what the creator has explicitly approved. This isn't just an ethics point, it's a direct liability and trust issue if a creator finds out their "voice" said something they never agreed to.

**Module 4 action item:** Write a one-page tone/boundaries guide template you'd use with a new creator.

## Module 5 — Contracts, Pricing & Getting Paid

**Lesson 5.1 — What every creator contract needs**
- Revenue share % and how/when it's calculated and paid
- Scope of services (growth only? chatting? content scheduling?)
- Confidentiality clause (protects the creator's private data/content)
- Termination clause (notice period, handover process for account access)
- Who owns what (content stays the creator's; your systems/processes stay yours)

A simple contract template is included as `contract-template.md` — adapt with a solicitor before using for real client money once revenue is meaningful.

**Lesson 5.2 — Getting paid**
Most agencies get paid by having the creator pay out the agency's cut directly (bank transfer/Wise) after the platform pays the creator — you generally won't have direct access to platform payout accounts, so build the payment cadence into the contract (e.g., "agency invoice sent monthly, paid within 7 days of creator's platform payout").

**Lesson 5.3 — Handling disputes**
Keep a shared revenue-tracking sheet visible to both sides so there's never ambiguity about what's owed. Most disputes come from opacity, not bad faith — fix that with transparency, not just trust.

**Module 5 action item:** Fill out the contract template with your actual terms before your first signed creator.

## Module 6 — Tools & Systems

**Lesson 6.1 — Core stack**
- Content scheduling: native platform scheduler or a third-party tool the creator approves
- Analytics: platform's built-in dashboard + your own tracking sheet (Google Sheets is fine to start)
- Communications: Discord/Telegram/Slack for internal team comms (not for the creator's actual subscriber chats)
- Payments/invoicing: Wise for international transfers, simple invoice via Wave or similar

**Lesson 6.2 — Documentation**
Keep a "creator playbook" doc per account: their tone guide, PPV price points, top-performing content types, boundaries, and current growth targets. This is what lets you hand shifts between chatters without dropping quality.

**Module 6 action item:** Set up your tracking sheet + creator playbook template before signing anyone.

## Module 7 — Scaling: Hiring a Team

**Lesson 7.1 — When to hire your first chatter**
Once one account consistently earns enough that your time is the bottleneck (you can't cover all active hours yourself), it's time to hire — not before. Hiring too early on unproven revenue is the most common way new agencies go broke.

**Lesson 7.2 — Hiring & training chatters**
- Hire based on writing ability, tone-matching in a test message exercise, and reliability (shift attendance matters more than raw sales skill at first).
- Run a paid trial shift under supervision before handing over full access.
- Pay structure: base rate per shift + small commission on PPV sales they close, so incentives align with the creator's earnings.

**Lesson 7.3 — Managing multiple accounts**
Standardize your systems (Module 6) so onboarding creator #2, #3, #4 is a repeatable process, not a from-scratch redesign every time.

**Module 7 action item:** Draft a one-page chatter job ad and a 3-question tone-matching test you'd use to screen applicants.

## Module 8 — Longevity: Legal, Reputation & Staying in Business

**Lesson 8.1 — Compliance basics (see legal-compliance-checklist.md for the full list)**
Every creator you manage must be independently platform-verified as an adult. Never advise or help anyone circumvent a platform's identity/age verification — that's an immediate, non-negotiable line, both ethically and legally.

**Lesson 8.2 — Reputation is the business**
This industry runs on referrals between creators. One bad-faith agency relationship (skimmed payments, broken boundaries, leaked content) ends up known fast. Transparent contracts and honest reporting are a growth strategy, not just risk management.

**Lesson 8.3 — Diversify, don't over-concentrate**
Don't let one creator become 80% of your revenue — a single account leaving should not sink the business. Reinvest early profit into signing a second and third account.

**Lesson 8.4 — Your next 90 days**
1. Finalize your service offer and contract (Modules 2 & 5)
2. Sign your first creator (Module 2)
3. Build your tracking + playbook systems (Module 6)
4. Hit a consistent growth/revenue rhythm before hiring anyone (Module 7)
5. Revisit the legal checklist quarterly as you scale

## Appendix: Contract template (starter, non-legal-advice)

```
CREATOR MANAGEMENT AGREEMENT (SIMPLE TEMPLATE)

Between: [Agency Name] ("Agency") and [Creator Name] ("Creator")
Date: [date]

1. Services: Agency will provide [growth marketing / chatting / content scheduling — list specifics] for Creator's account on [platform].

2. Compensation: Creator agrees to pay Agency [X]% of gross revenue generated during the term of this agreement, calculated monthly and paid within [7] days of Creator's platform payout.

3. Term: This agreement begins on [date] and continues until terminated by either party with [14] days written notice.

4. Confidentiality: Agency will keep all Creator content, financial data, and subscriber information confidential and will not share, sell, or use it outside the scope of this agreement.

5. Account Access & Handover: Upon termination, Agency will immediately return all account access and delete any locally stored Creator data within [7] days.

6. Boundaries: Agency and any staff acting on Creator's behalf will only communicate within the tone/boundaries guide agreed in writing with Creator (Exhibit A), and will not make offers or statements Creator has not approved.

7. Independent verification: Creator confirms they are an adult, independently verified per platform requirements, and Agency will not assist in circumventing any platform verification process for Creator or any other individual.

Signed: _______________________ (Agency)   Date: _______
Signed: _______________________ (Creator)  Date: _______
```
*(Have an actual solicitor review/adapt this before using it for real signed deals — this is a starting skeleton, not legal advice.)*
