# Legal & Compliance Checklist

This isn't optional reading — this is the difference between a real business and something that gets your Instagram account, Skool store, or Stripe account banned in week one. Go through this before you post the first Reel.

**This is general information, not legal advice.** Get a real accountant/solicitor for your specific country/state before you take real money — this checklist tells you what to ask them about, it doesn't replace them.

---

## 1. Business structure

- [ ] Register a business entity (in Australia: ABN as sole trader is enough to start; consider a company structure once revenue is meaningful for liability separation).
- [ ] Open a separate business bank account — never mix course revenue with personal spending, makes tax time a nightmare and looks bad if ever audited.
- [ ] Decide GST registration threshold (AU: mandatory once turnover > $75k/yr) and register in time.
- [ ] Put the profit split with your mate **in writing** — a simple signed agreement stating: percentage split, who owns the Skool/brand/IP if you ever part ways, what happens if one person stops contributing. A one-page doc signed by both of you is enough at this stage; doesn't need a lawyer for v1, but get one once revenue is real.

## 2. Course/product compliance

- [ ] **No guaranteed-income claims.** Never say "you will make $X." Say "here's what I/my mate did, here's the method — your results depend on your effort/market."
- [ ] Any earnings screenshots or numbers must be real, and ideally dated/verifiable. Keep records in case anyone asks.
- [ ] Include an **earnings disclaimer** on the sales page and inside the course (template below).
- [ ] Include **Terms of Sale** (refund policy, what's included, no resale of course materials) — Skool has basic templates you can adapt.
- [ ] 18+ only: the course itself, the community, and (implicitly) the underlying business only deals with consenting adult creators on adult platforms. State this plainly wherever you sell.

**Earnings disclaimer template:**
> This course provides business education and is not a guarantee of income. Results depend on individual effort, market conditions, and factors outside our control. Any income examples shown are real results from the instructor's own experience and are not typical or promised outcomes for every student.

## 3. Advertising / platform ToS (this is the actual risk area)

- [ ] **Instagram/Meta:** Do not depict, imply, or link to adult/sexual content in ads or organic posts. Meta's policies prohibit ads/content that facilitates or promotes adult services, even indirectly (e.g., "how to sell nudes" framing will get flagged). Frame everything as **business/marketing/agency education** — talking-head advice, dashboards, business growth tips, entrepreneurship content. This is not just a legal nicety, it's the difference between the account surviving or getting shadowbanned/removed.
- [ ] Avoid explicit platform names in ad copy if running paid ads — organic posts have more leeway, but paid Meta ads reviewers are stricter about anything adult-adjacent.
- [ ] **Skool** ToS: read their content policy — Skool generally allows business/education communities; keep all public-facing community content (previews, free posts) non-explicit, business-only.
- [ ] **Payment processors (Stripe, via Skool):** Stripe's acceptable use policy restricts certain "high-risk" adult-adjacent categories. Selling a *course about* agency management is generally fine (it's an education product, not an adult content sale), but avoid language that makes it look like you're selling access to adult content itself. Keep invoices/product descriptions squarely about "business coaching / educational course."

## 4. If the agency side (stream A) is real and operating

Even though the course is the product you're building right now, the curriculum will teach the actual agency model — so it should model compliant practices, both because it's right and because a course that teaches shady practices is a liability magnet:

- [ ] **Creator contracts**: written agreement with every managed creator covering revenue share %, scope of work, confidentiality, termination terms, and content ownership. Never touch a creator's account without a signed agreement.
- [ ] **Age & identity verification**: every creator managed must be independently verified 18+ and account-verified per the platform's own KYC (OnlyFans requires ID verification at signup — never manage or advise circumventing this).
- [ ] **No account-sharing password practices that violate platform ToS** without the creator's informed, revocable consent and a clear off-boarding process (return access, delete stored data).
- [ ] **Data handling**: any private messages, financial data or content the agency touches should be covered by a basic privacy/confidentiality clause in the creator contract.
- [ ] **Chatters/staff**: if hiring people to chat as the creator, they need clear contracts too, plus guidance to never impersonate the creator in ways the creator hasn't authorized, and never engage in anything illegal (no soliciting/facilitating anything outside consenting adult content — e.g., no engagement with anyone who appears underage, immediate termination of any account/chat where age is in doubt, full compliance with each platform's law-enforcement/CSAM reporting obligations).

## 5. Tax

- [ ] Course revenue is ordinary income — declare it.
- [ ] Keep receipts for course-build costs (Skool sub, Canva, equipment) — deductible business expenses.
- [ ] Set aside ~25-30% of profit for tax from day one so BAS/tax time isn't a shock.

## 6. Simple pre-launch checklist

- [ ] Business entity registered
- [ ] Bank account separated
- [ ] Written split agreement with your mate signed
- [ ] Skool Terms of Sale + refund policy published
- [ ] Earnings disclaimer on sales page + inside course
- [ ] Instagram content plan reviewed for ad-policy safety (no adult content/imagery)
- [ ] Every claim in the sales page is true and can be backed up
