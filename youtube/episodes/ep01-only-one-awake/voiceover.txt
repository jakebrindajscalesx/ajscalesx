=== CLIP 01: 1. Hook (0:00) ===
At three-twelve A M, the whole world falls asleep. Except you. By day 3, you'll find out why nobody can wake up, and why you never should have tried.

=== CLIP 02: 2. The turn (0:14) ===
It starts with something small. You wake up thirsty. You walk to the kitchen, pour a glass of water, and you notice it: the fridge hum, the traffic outside, the neighbor's TV. Everything is still running. It's just that nobody is doing anything.
<break time="0.8s" />
So here's what we're going to do. We're going to follow you, hour by hour, for the next 72 hours, and see exactly how long the world keeps going without anyone awake to run it.

=== CLIP 03: 3. Hour 1: The house (0:50) ===
You go to your brother's room first. You say his name. Then you shout it. Then you shake him, hard. He's breathing. His eyes are moving under his eyelids like he's dreaming. But he won't wake up.
<break time="0.8s" />
Your parents are the same. Your mom fell asleep mid-scroll, phone still in her hand. You try cold water. You try an alarm right next to her ear. You try the thing you swore you'd never do: you pinch her, hard.
<break time="0.8s" />
Nothing.
<break time="0.8s" />
You call nine-one-one. It rings. And rings. And rings. Somewhere, in a dispatch center, a phone is lighting up on a desk in front of someone who is fast asleep.

=== CLIP 04: 4. Hour 2: The street (1:43) ===
You go outside. It's quieter than any city should be. A car has drifted into a mailbox at the end of the street, engine still running, the driver slumped over the wheel. Asleep. Not hurt. Just gone.
<break time="0.8s" />
Gas station clerk. Asleep. Security guard. Asleep. But here's the first weird thing: the dogs are awake. Every animal is awake. It's only people.
<break time="0.8s" />
Whatever this is, it happened to humans. All of them. All at once.

=== CLIP 05: 5. Hour 6: The sky (2:21) ===
By sunrise you're on a rooftop looking up, and you see a plane. It's flying perfectly straight, perfectly level. And that's what scares you.
<break time="0.8s" />
Planes don't need pilots to stay in the air. The autopilot will hold its course and altitude for hours. The problem is it'll keep holding them until the fuel runs out. This has actually happened before: in 2005, a passenger jet over Greece flew on autopilot for hours after its crew passed out, until the fuel ran out.
<break time="0.8s" />
Right now there are thousands of planes in the sky. And every single one of them has a clock on it.
<break time="0.8s" />
You don't watch the end. You can't.

=== CLIP 06: 6. Hour 14: The lights (3:12) ===
At hour 14, the lights start going out.
<break time="0.8s" />
The power grid isn't a machine that runs itself. People balance it all day, every day, matching how much power gets made to how much gets used. With nobody at the controls, small faults stop getting fixed, and they start to spread.
<break time="0.8s" />
But one building stays lit. The hospital. Backup generators. And that's when the real problem hits you, the one that's been sitting under everything else.

=== CLIP 07: 7. Hour 20: The clock (3:48) ===
Remember that glass of water? You woke up because you were thirsty. Everyone else is going to be thirsty too, and they can't get up to drink.
<break time="0.8s" />
A person can usually survive about three days without water. That means you don't have forever to figure this out. You have 72 hours, and 20 of them are already gone.
<break time="0.8s" />
So you make a decision. You can't save everyone. But maybe you can save them.

=== CLIP 08: 8. Day 2: The hospital (4:23) ===
Getting three sleeping people to a hospital, alone, takes you nine hours, a shopping cart, a wheelchair, and one very bad decision involving a delivery van you definitely don't know how to drive.
<break time="0.8s" />
The nurses are asleep at their stations. You find a poster on the wall about setting up an IV drip, and you watch a training video on one of the computers, over and over, until your hands stop shaking.
<break time="0.8s" />
By midnight, your family is in three beds, on fluids, breathing steady. You bought them time. Not a cure. Time.

=== CLIP 09: 9. Day 2, night: The phone (5:06) ===
That's when your phone rings.
<break time="0.8s" />
You stare at it. Every phone on Earth is ringing right now, because nobody's answering. But this one's calling you.
<break time="0.8s" />
You pick up. Static. And then, under the static, a voice. You can't make out the words, only that it's a woman's voice, and that she sounds like she's been crying.
<break time="0.8s" />
The line cuts. You call back. This number is not in service.

=== CLIP 10: 10. Day 3: Waking one (5:40) ===
Day 3. You've read everything you can find about sleep. And there's one thing you haven't tried: not noise, not pain. Something familiar. Something only that person would recognize.
<break time="0.8s" />
You play your brother a song. The stupid one you two always fought over in the car. You hum the part he always got wrong.
<break time="0.8s" />
His eyes open.
<break time="0.8s" />
He looks right at you. And he says the strangest thing anyone has ever said to you:
<break time="0.8s" />
"Why are you here? You're supposed to be the one in the bed."

=== CLIP 11: 11. The twist (6:23) ===
And the walls start to melt.
<break time="0.8s" />
Not fall. Melt, like wet paint. The ward, the beds, the window, the city behind it. Because you finally understand why nobody could wake up.
<break time="0.8s" />
They were never asleep. You were.
<break time="0.8s" />
At three-twelve A M, three days ago, you got up for a glass of water, and you never made it back to bed. Everything since then, the empty streets, the planes, the lights going out, has been happening inside your own head. And the voice on the phone was your mom, sitting next to you, holding the phone to your ear because a doctor told her familiar voices might help.
<break time="0.8s" />
And that's why you never should have tried to wake them. Every time you reached one of them, you were really reaching for the way out. And the way out means leaving this world behind, the one where you were the only one who mattered, and going back to the one where you're the one who needs help.

=== CLIP 12: 12. The payoff and close (7:37) ===
So here's the choice the whole video has been building to. Stay in a world where you're the only one awake, a world that's slowly running out of power, water and people. Or take his hand, and wake up into a hospital bed, with a long recovery and everyone you love crying at your bedside.
<break time="0.8s" />
You take his hand.
<break time="0.8s" />
And somewhere, in a real hospital, at three-twelve in the afternoon, a phone hits the floor.
<break time="0.8s" />
Tell me in the comments: would you have stayed? And if you want to see what happens when the opposite happens, when everyone else wakes up and you're the one who can't, that's the next one.
