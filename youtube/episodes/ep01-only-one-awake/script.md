# Script: What IF You Were the Only One Awake for 72 Hours?

Format: second-person "What IF" story (the IFOLOGY-style format: one impossible premise, followed
all the way through, told to "you"). Fully animated, narrated, no face on camera.

Runtime estimate: ~1,070 spoken words at 150 wpm, plus scene pauses = **about 8:30 of narration**.
With 2-3 second visual holds on the big beats, expect a **9-10 minute** cut. To hit 12+ minutes
(IFOLOGY's longer episodes run 18-25), expand Day 2 with a second rescue attempt.

---

| **1 (use this)** | At 3:12 a.m., the whole world falls asleep. Except you. By day 3, you'll find out why nobody can wake up, and why you never should have tried. | **73 STRONG** | The Impossible Claim |
| 2 (backup) | What if you were the last person awake on Earth, and every hour you fail to wake someone, 300,000 people never open their eyes again? | 71 WORKABLE | The Question |

Hook 1 wins because it opens two questions (*why can't they wake?* and *why shouldn't you try?*),
and the video answers both in the last two minutes. Hook 2's "300,000" is a story number, not a
real statistic. Only use it if the on-screen text makes clear it's part of the fiction.

## The script

Beat markers: `[ON SCREEN: ...]` says what the animation shows. `(0:00)` is the approximate
timecode at 150 wpm.

### 1. Hook (0:00)

[ON SCREEN: Black. A digital clock flips to 3:12. Hard cut to a silent bedroom lit only by the clock.]

At 3:12 a.m., the whole world falls asleep. Except you. By day 3, you'll find out why nobody can
wake up, and why you never should have tried.

### 2. The turn (0:14)

[ON SCREEN: You, the character, sitting up in bed, rubbing your eyes. Title card: "HOUR 0".]

It starts with something small. You wake up thirsty. You walk to the kitchen, pour a glass of
water, and you notice it: the fridge hum, the traffic outside, the neighbor's TV. Everything is
still running. It's just that nobody is doing anything.

So here's what we're going to do. We're going to follow you, hour by hour, for the next 72 hours,
and see exactly how long the world keeps going without anyone awake to run it.

### 3. Hour 1: The house (0:50)

[ON SCREEN: Hallway. You shake your brother's shoulder. Nothing. Close-up: he's breathing, peaceful, eyes moving under the lids.]

You go to your brother's room first. You say his name. Then you shout it. Then you shake him,
hard. He's breathing. His eyes are moving under his eyelids like he's dreaming. But he won't wake
up.

[ON SCREEN: Parents' room. Your mom is asleep with her phone still in her hand, screen glowing.]

Your parents are the same. Your mom fell asleep mid-scroll, phone still in her hand. You try cold
water. You try an alarm right next to her ear. You try the thing you swore you'd never do: you
pinch her, hard.

Nothing.

[ON SCREEN: You on the kitchen floor, phone to your ear. "Calling 911…" Ringing. Ringing. Ringing.]

You call 911. It rings. And rings. And rings. Somewhere, in a dispatch center, a phone is lighting
up on a desk in front of someone who is fast asleep.

### 4. Hour 2: The street (1:43)

[ON SCREEN: Wide shot of your street at night. A car has rolled gently into a mailbox, engine still running, headlights on.]

You go outside. It's quieter than any city should be. A car has drifted into a mailbox at the end
of the street, engine still running, the driver slumped over the wheel. Asleep. Not hurt. Just gone.

[ON SCREEN: Montage: a night-shift worker asleep at a gas station counter; a security guard asleep in front of monitors; a dog awake, whining at its sleeping owner.]

Gas station clerk. Asleep. Security guard. Asleep. But here's the first weird thing: the dogs are
awake. Every animal is awake. It's only people.

Whatever this is, it happened to humans. All of them. All at once.

### 5. Hour 6: The sky (2:21)

[ON SCREEN: You on a rooftop at dawn, staring up. A contrail crosses the sky, perfectly straight.]

By sunrise you're on a rooftop looking up, and you see a plane. It's flying perfectly straight,
perfectly level. And that's what scares you.

[ON SCREEN: Cutaway diagram: cockpit, two sleeping pilots, the autopilot light glowing green. Fuel gauge ticking down.]

Planes don't need pilots to stay in the air. The autopilot will hold its course and altitude for
hours. The problem is it'll keep holding them until the fuel runs out. This has actually happened
before: in 2005, a passenger jet over Greece flew on autopilot for hours after its crew passed out,
until the fuel ran out. *(Check: Helios Airways Flight 522.)*

Right now there are thousands of planes in the sky. And every single one of them has a clock on it.

[ON SCREEN: The contrail ends. Silence. Hold for two seconds. No crash shown, just the line stopping.]

You don't watch the end. You can't.

### 6. Hour 14: The lights (3:12)

[ON SCREEN: Title card: "HOUR 14". City skyline at dusk. One district blinks out. Then another.]

At hour 14, the lights start going out.

The power grid isn't a machine that runs itself. People balance it all day, every day, matching how
much power gets made to how much gets used. With nobody at the controls, small faults stop getting
fixed, and they start to spread. *(Check: grid timing for an unstaffed failure.)*

[ON SCREEN: You running through a darkening street, the glow of a hospital sign still on in the distance.]

But one building stays lit. The hospital. Backup generators. And that's when the real problem hits
you, the one that's been sitting under everything else.

### 7. Hour 20: The clock (3:48)

[ON SCREEN: Close-up on the glass of water you poured at 3:12 a.m., still on the kitchen counter in your memory. A timer appears: 72:00:00, already counting down from 52.]

Remember that glass of water? You woke up because you were thirsty. Everyone else is going to be
thirsty too, and they can't get up to drink.

A person can usually survive about three days without water. *(Check: the commonly cited
"rule of threes"; it varies with heat and health.)* That means you don't have forever to figure
this out. You have 72 hours, and 20 of them are already gone.

[ON SCREEN: Split screen: your sleeping family on the left, a hospital supply room on the right.]

So you make a decision. You can't save everyone. But maybe you can save them.

### 8. Day 2: The hospital (4:23)

[ON SCREEN: Title card: "DAY 2". You dragging your mom in a wheelbarrow, then a shopping cart, then a borrowed wheelchair. Comedy beat, but the music stays tense.]

Getting three sleeping people to a hospital, alone, takes you nine hours, a shopping cart, a
wheelchair, and one very bad decision involving a delivery van you definitely don't know how to drive.

[ON SCREEN: Hospital ward. Rows of sleeping nurses at their stations. You reading a laminated poster: "How to set up an IV."]

The nurses are asleep at their stations. You find a poster on the wall about setting up an IV
drip, and you watch a training video on one of the computers, over and over, until your hands stop
shaking.

[ON SCREEN: Your family in three beds, IV lines in, monitors beeping. You collapse into a chair.]

By midnight, your family is in three beds, on fluids, breathing steady. You bought them time. Not
a cure. Time.

### 9. Day 2, night: The phone (5:06)

[ON SCREEN: The ward, dark except for the monitors. Your phone buzzes on the bedside table. Unknown number.]

That's when your phone rings.

You stare at it. Every phone on Earth is ringing right now, because nobody's answering. But this
one's calling *you*.

[ON SCREEN: You answer. Static. A voice, faint and warped, like it's coming from very far away.]

You pick up. Static. And then, under the static, a voice. You can't make out the words, only that
it's a woman's voice, and that she sounds like she's been crying.

The line cuts. You call back. *This number is not in service.*

### 10. Day 3: Waking one (5:40)

[ON SCREEN: Title card: "DAY 3". You at a library computer, reading articles about sleep, coma, brain waves. Notes everywhere.]

Day 3. You've read everything you can find about sleep. And there's one thing you haven't tried:
not noise, not pain. Something familiar. Something only that person would recognize.

[ON SCREEN: You at your brother's bedside, playing a song on your phone. The song you two always argued about in the car.]

You play your brother a song. The stupid one you two always fought over in the car. You hum the
part he always got wrong.

[ON SCREEN: His finger twitches. His eyes open. He looks at you.]

His eyes open.

He looks right at you. And he says the strangest thing anyone has ever said to you:

**"Why are you here? You're supposed to be the one in the bed."**

### 11. The twist (6:23)

[ON SCREEN: The hospital ward starts to dissolve at the edges, like wet paint. Your brother's face flickers.]

And the walls start to melt.

Not fall. Melt, like wet paint. The ward, the beds, the window, the city behind it. Because you
finally understand why nobody could wake up.

[ON SCREEN: Flashback montage, but now from the outside: a real hospital room, daylight. You are in the bed. Your family around you. Your mom on the phone, crying. The clock on the wall: 3:12.]

They were never asleep. You were.

At 3:12 a.m., three days ago, you got up for a glass of water, and you never made it back to bed.
Everything since then, the empty streets, the planes, the lights going out, has been happening
inside your own head. And the voice on the phone was your mom, sitting next to you, holding the
phone to your ear because a doctor told her familiar voices might help.

[ON SCREEN: Back in the dream. Your brother, standing now, holding out his hand.]

And that's why you never should have tried to wake them. Every time you reached one of them, you
were really reaching for the way out. And the way out means leaving this world behind, the one
where you were the only one who mattered, and going back to the one where you're the one who
needs help.

### 12. The payoff and close (7:37)

[ON SCREEN: You look at your brother's hand. The empty, beautiful, silent city behind you. Then the hand.]

So here's the choice the whole video has been building to. Stay in a world where you're the only
one awake, a world that's slowly running out of power, water and people. Or take his hand, and
wake up into a hospital bed, with a long recovery and everyone you love crying at your bedside.

[ON SCREEN: You take the hand. White flash. Real hospital room. Your eyes open. Your mom drops the phone.]

You take his hand.

And somewhere, in a real hospital, at 3:12 in the afternoon, a phone hits the floor.

[ON SCREEN: End card: "WHAT IF… everyone else woke up and you didn't?" with the next episode's thumbnail.]

Tell me in the comments: would you have stayed? And if you want to see what happens when the
opposite happens, when everyone else wakes up and you're the one who can't, that's the next one.

## Notes for production

- **On-screen fact flags:** before you record, check every line marked *(Check: …)*. They're
  real-world anchors and should be right. Everything else is fiction.
- **Never show a crash.** The plane beat ends on the contrail stopping. That keeps the video
  advertiser-friendly and makes it scarier.
- **One ask at the close.** The comment question doubles as the setup for episode 2.
